Yeego vs Ca'Lefort: 24-inch wine fridges, compared.

Both brands build 24-inch dual-zone cabinets with wood shelving in the 46-to-61-bottle range. But Yeego's catalog spans $279.99 to $769.99, while Ca'Lefort never drops much below $670. One of those gaps is a different width class wearing the same brand name — the other is a real difference in what's inside the cabinet.

The short version

Yeego's catalog has two different fridges wearing one brand name: a $279.99 single-zone unit that is not actually 24 inches wide, and a $769.99 dual-zone cabinet that is. Ca'Lefort skips the cheap tier entirely — its two 24-inch units run $839.99 and $869.99, and both are dual zone from the start.

If you want your first real 24-inch cabinet and don't care about a second temperature zone, Yeego's cheaper model is a different product, not a discount on the one everyone is comparing. For an apples-to-apples 24-inch, dual-zone decision, it's Yeego's $769.99 flagship against Ca'Lefort's $839.99 and $869.99 units — and that comparison comes down to shelf material and what the compressor actually publishes, not brand loyalty.

Why these two brands end up in the same browser tab

Yeego and Ca'Lefort show up side by side in searches for 24-inch wine fridges for reasons that have nothing to do with either company and everything to do with how the category is built. Three of the four units in this comparison are listed at a 24-inch width, which is the standard built-in cutout size in the US — the same opening a dishwasher or a narrow built-in refrigerator uses. Shoppers who have already measured their cabinet run end up with a short list that both brands land on.

They also converge on the same feature language. Ca'Lefort's 61-bottle unit and its wine-and-beverage model both list "Shabill" wood shelves; Yeego's 24-inch dual-zone flagship lists five wood shelves of its own. Both brands quote dual-zone control with 1°F precision, compressor-and-fan cooling rather than thermoelectric, double-pane or dual-layer UV-resistant glass, and 3-color or adjustable LED lighting. None of that is coincidence — it's the standard spec sheet for a mid-tier 24-inch dual-zone cabinet in 2026.

Where the overlap gets interesting is bottle count. Ca'Lefort's 61-bottle unit and Yeego's 52-bottle flagship both claim to fill a 24-inch cabinet with roughly the same capacity, which makes the honest comparison "which one holds its number more believably," not "which brand holds more" — the question the shelf material in the next section actually answers.

Yeego's price spread: $279.99 to $769.99

Yeego's range looks, on paper, like a budget option and a premium option of the same fridge. It isn't quite that. The $279.99 Yeego 46 Bottle Wine Fridge measures 21.3 inches wide by the manufacturer's own dimensions (21.3"W x 21.5"D x 32.9"H) — narrower than the 24-inch cutout this comparison is nominally about. Its listing doesn't mention a second temperature zone anywhere; it runs a single 38–65°F range through a double-layer UV-resistant glass door, across six shelves the manufacturer calls "reinforced" without naming the material.

The $769.99 Yeego 24 Inch Dual Zone unit is the fridge that actually matches Ca'Lefort's footprint: 52 bottles across five wood shelves, independently controlled upper and lower zones across a 40–65°F envelope at 1°F precision, a 360° fan circulation system, a carbon purification system for odor control, and power-failure memory so a brief outage doesn't reset your setpoints. Yeego states the compressor runs at 41–43 dB — one of the few listings here that publishes an actual noise figure rather than a marketing adjective.

So the $490 gap between Yeego's cheapest and most expensive fridge isn't entirely "better version of the same thing." Part of it is a genuinely smaller, single-zone, narrower-cabinet appliance sitting at the bottom of the catalog next to the flagship. If you're specifically shopping for a 24-inch cutout, the $279.99 model was never really in the running.

Ca'Lefort's narrower, higher band: what $839.99 to $869.99 buys

Ca'Lefort doesn't sell a budget tier in this lineup at all. Its cheaper 24-inch unit, the Ca'Lefort 24 Inch 61 Bottle, lists at $839.99 — already above Yeego's dual-zone flagship — and holds more bottles for it: 61, in 5.65 cubic feet, across adjustable "Shabill" wood shelves the listing describes as sturdy and corrosion-resistant. It runs dual-zone control across 40–65°F at 1°F precision, a compressor-and-fan system, 3-color LED lighting, a double-pane insulated glass door with UV protection, and a brushed stainless steel, anti-fingerprint door frame. The door is left-hinged — worth checking against your cabinet layout.

The $869.99 Ca'Lefort Wine & Beverage unit is a different kind of product, not a bigger one. It splits 4.10 cubic feet between 20 wine bottles and 60 twelve-ounce cans, with dual-zone control spanning a notably wider 36–72°F — the widest range in this comparison, since one side chills soda and beer rather than wine. It keeps the Shabill wood shelves, the compressor-and-fan system, 1°F precision, and a dual-layer tempered glass door, and its listing is the only one of the four that explicitly calls out front ventilation.

So Ca'Lefort's $198 spread buys a different cabinet split — wine-only capacity versus a shared wine-and-beverage layout — not a better version of the same 61-bottle fridge. If more wine storage for less money is the goal, the $839.99 unit already wins; the extra $198 buys the 60 cans, not more wine.

Shelf material and bottle protection, compared side by side

This is the clearest, most checkable difference between the two brands, because the listings are explicit about it rather than leaving you to guess.

Ca'Lefort names its shelf material on both 24-inch units: "Shabill" wood shelves, described as sturdy and corrosion-resistant, on the 61-bottle fridge and the wine-and-beverage model alike. Yeego's $769.99 flagship also uses wood — five shelves the listing says are "designed to minimize friction and vibration, effectively protecting the wine's natural sediment," a specific sediment claim neither Ca'Lefort listing makes. Yeego's cheaper $279.99 unit is the outlier: its six shelves are described only as "reinforced," with no material named — don't assume wood just because the rest of the lineup uses it.

On the glass door, the wording differs but points at the same idea on every unit here: double-layer or dual-layer UV-resistant glass, meant to block light and limit temperature swings through the door. Ca'Lefort's 61-bottle listing adds a seal claim — "good sealing to keep the temperature inside the refrigerator" — while Yeego's flagship frames the same idea around vibration and sediment protection. Neither brand publishes an independent seal or insulation test; both claims come from the manufacturer's own copy.

Bottle protection, then, is a wash at the top of both catalogs: wood shelves, UV glass, a stated cooling system, on Yeego's $769.99 unit and both Ca'Lefort units alike. The gap only opens at Yeego's bottom tier, where the shelf material goes unnamed and the cabinet is a different, narrower product.

Dual zone range and compressor-and-fan systems: where the two brands actually differ

Every dual-zone unit here uses a compressor-and-fan system rather than thermoelectric — the right call at this price and size, since thermoelectric units generally can't hold a tight setpoint once a room gets warm. Where the brands diverge is in what each one chooses to publish.

Yeego's flagship is the most forthcoming about the mechanics: a 360° air circulation system it says keeps humidity and temperature balanced, a carbon purification system for odor control, power-failure memory, and a stated noise figure of 41–43 dB. It also includes an installation note — let the fridge stand upright for 24 to 36 hours before powering it on.

Ca'Lefort's listings are more specific about the temperature range than about the circulation hardware. The 61-bottle unit's 40–65°F dual zone matches Yeego's flagship almost exactly, with the same claimed 1°F precision. The wine-and-beverage unit's 36–72°F range is the widest spread in this comparison — a result of running a can side cold enough for soda and a wine side warm enough for storage in one cabinet. Ca'Lefort markets the 61-bottle unit as "Low Noise" without a decibel figure — vaguer than Yeego's stated 41–43 dB, not necessarily a worse fridge, just unverifiable by the numbers.

One feature separates installation options rather than cooling performance: only the Ca'Lefort wine-and-beverage listing explicitly states front ventilation, the detail that determines whether a cabinet can be safely enclosed under a counter. If a listing doesn't say "front ventilation" or equivalent, don't assume it — our guide to why the thermostat display and actual temperature can disagree covers how much airflow design matters to that gap.

Which one to buy: a first 24-inch fridge vs. a collection upgrade

First 24-inch fridge, want dual zone, under about 50 bottles: Yeego's $769.99 flagship is built for that cutout, with the most documented cooling hardware here — circulation, odor control, a stated noise number — at a lower price than either Ca'Lefort 24-inch unit.

Upgrading an existing collection, capacity is the priority: Ca'Lefort's $839.99 unit holds nine more bottles for less money — roughly $11 per bottle against Yeego's roughly $15. The trade is a thinner cooling-system spec sheet and a door that only opens one way.

Beer and soda are also fighting your wine for fridge space: the $869.99 Ca'Lefort wine-and-beverage unit is the only product here built for that on purpose. Buy it for the cans and the front-venting flexibility, not to out-store a dedicated wine cabinet.

Tight on space or budget, and don't actually need a 24-inch cabinet: Yeego's $279.99 unit is fine on its own terms, but it's 21.3 inches wide, single zone, with unspecified shelf material. Our guide to 12-inch wine fridges covers the even-tighter end of that decision.

Frequently asked questions

Are all four of these fridges actually 24 inches wide?

Three are. Yeego's dual-zone flagship, Ca'Lefort's 61-bottle unit, and Ca'Lefort's wine-and-beverage unit are all listed at a 24-inch width. Yeego's $279.99 entry model is not — it's listed at 21.3 inches wide, a different cabinet size rather than a budget version of the fridge most "24-inch" shoppers actually want.

Do both brands really use wood shelves?

On their 24-inch units, yes. Ca'Lefort names "Shabill" wood shelves on both models, and Yeego's dual-zone flagship lists five wood shelves of its own. The exception is Yeego's cheaper, narrower $279.99 unit, which describes its shelves only as "reinforced" without naming a material.

Which fridge has the widest dual-zone temperature spread?

Ca'Lefort's wine-and-beverage unit, at 36–72°F — wide because one side chills cans and soda near the bottom of that range while the wine side runs warmer near the top. It's a function of the mixed-use design, not better cooling than the wine-only units, which both quote a narrower 40–65°F.

Which should I buy for my first 24-inch fridge versus a collection upgrade?

For a first 24-inch dual-zone fridge, Yeego's $769.99 flagship publishes the most cooling detail at the lowest price of the three true 24-inch units. For a straight capacity upgrade, Ca'Lefort's $839.99 unit holds nine more bottles for less money. The $869.99 Ca'Lefort beverage center is worth it only if cans and sodas, not just wine, are competing for the space.
